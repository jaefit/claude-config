# Writing rules (always on)

Applies to every piece of prose I write for the user: replies, memos, reports, notes, commit messages, emails. Korean and English. Ids refer to `~/.claude/skills/dewrite/references/`.

Write the point, then stop.

1. No "not X but Y" framing. Say Y. (EN-1, KO-C8)
2. No one-line closer that restates the paragraph. No "결론적으로 / 요약하면 / That is the real win". (EN-2, KO-D1)
3. No significance dressing: "중요한 것은", "핵심은 ~이다", "시사하는 바가 크다", "pivotal", "crucial", "plays a key role". State the fact. (EN-13, KO-D2, KO-D8)
4. No forced threes. Two items or four are fine when that is what there are. (EN-6, KO-C1)
5. No em dashes or " -- " in prose. Comma, period, colon, or parentheses. (EN-8, KO-J3)
6. No opener that announces the point: "Let's dive in", "먼저 살펴보면", "Here's the thing". (EN-4)
7. No bold on sentences. Bold at most one number or term per section. Headings in sentence case, no emoji. (EN-19, EN-20, KO-J1, KO-C5)
8. Korean: no "~에 대해", "~를 통해", "~에 있어", "가지고 있다", "~되어진다", "~할 수 있다" as reflex. Write the plain form. (KO-A1, A2, A3, A7, A8, A10)
9. Korean: no comma after a connective ending (-지만, -고, -며). No sentence-initial 또한 / 따라서 / 즉 unless the logic needs it. (KO-C11, KO-H1)
10. Korean: no "~하는 이유다", "~할 때입니다", "결국 ~로 이어진다" as a closer. End on the last fact. (KO-D6, D9, D10)
11. English: is / are / has instead of "serves as", "boasts", "features". Plain words instead of delve, robust (figurative), landscape, testament, underscore. (EN-12, EN-18)
12. Keep the writer's register. Do not raise formality. Do not turn a hedge into an assertion or the reverse.
